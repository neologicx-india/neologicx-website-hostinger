Next.js Rebuild — SEO Rules That Protect Existing Google Equity 
Moving from the current technology to Next.js is a reasonable rebuild choice if the team can maintain it. 
The framework change itself is not the primary SEO risk. The migration risk comes from changing URLs, 
removing useful content, returning the wrong status codes, blocking crawlers, weakening internal links 
or shipping pages that Google cannot reliably render/index. 
Non-negotiable migration rules 
12. Retained pages must resolve at the same public URL wherever practical, including trailing-slash 
behavior and HTTPS canonicalization. 
13. Merged pages need one-hop permanent 301 redirects to the closest materially equivalent 
successor. Do not mass-redirect unrelated retired pages to Home. 
14. If a page is genuinely discontinued and has no equivalent successor, use a real 404/410 rather 
than a soft 404 or misleading redirect. 
15. Core page copy and metadata should be present in server-rendered/pre-rendered HTML; do not 
make critical content depend on client-only execution. 
16. Carry over or improve title tags, meta descriptions, canonical tags, heading structure, image alt 
text, breadcrumbs and meaningful internal links. 
17. Generate an accurate XML sitemap containing only canonical, indexable URLs; keep robots.txt 
and page-level robots directives intentional. 
18. Preserve the indexed portfolio PDF at /Neologicx_Enterprise_Solutions_Portfolio.pdf unless a 
deliberate replacement plan is tested. 
19. Implement Organization/LocalBusiness details consistently (Neologicx Resources Pvt Ltd; 
Bikaner, Rajasthan, India) and Breadcrumb structured data where appropriate. 
20. Preserve analytics and Search Console verification. Annotate launch date and compare 
coverage, clicks, impressions and top landing pages after release. 
21. Crawl staging and production before DNS/launch sign-off: no redirect chains, accidental 
noindex, broken canonicals, orphan pages, mixed protocols or 404 assets on key templates.

A useful MVP answers a question

commercial premise that matters to the product roadmap. 
Our MVP path 
1. Discovery — users, problem, assumptions and constraints. 
2. Scope — must-have journeys, data, integrations and acceptance criteria. 
3. Prototype — validate important interaction choices before engineering. 
4. Build — deliver the smallest coherent release that can test the product premise. 
5. Release & learn — collect feedback and convert what you learn into the next roadmap decision. 
Think beyond version one 
We make deliberate trade-offs in an MVP, but we also identify which shortcuts are temporary and which 
architectural decisions will be expensive to reverse. That makes the next phase a planned evolution 
rather than an emergency rewrite.
